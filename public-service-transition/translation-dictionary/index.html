Translation Dictionary

Military and Public-Sector Terms in Private-Sector Language

Agard Research Associates Inc.

The Translation Dictionary explains 90 terms that veterans and public employees use every day and that private-sector employers often do not recognize. Each entry gives the meaning in government and a plain way to say it in the private sector, with its part of speech, private-sector synonyms where they help, and references. Search for a term or an acronym, jump to a letter, or choose a category. The full list of references is under the References tab.

The dictionary is part of the Public Service Transition Program and works alongside the Skills Translation Worksheet and the Résumé and CV Guide in the program materials.

This resource provides general information only and does not constitute legal, financial, or benefits advice. Rules change, so confirm details with the agency named in each source. Agard Research Associates Inc. is nonpartisan and does not support or oppose any political party, candidate, or legislation.

30 Percent or More Disabled Veteran authority

proper noun Federal workforce

Also: 30% authority

  1. In government: An authority that lets an agency appoint, without competition, any veteran with a service-connected disability of 30 percent or more. The appointment can later be converted to a permanent one.
  2. In the private sector: A direct-hire pathway, not a private-sector term.

References: 80

A

Antideficiency Act

proper noun Budget and finance
  1. In government: A law that bars agencies from obligating or spending more than, or before, the amounts available in an appropriation.
  2. In the private sector: Spending-control compliance. Experience keeping programs inside legal funding limits translates to budget control and audit readiness.Synonyms: spending control

References: 67

APEX Accelerator

noun Contracting

Also: PTAC

  1. In government: Defense Department-funded centers, formerly called Procurement Technical Assistance Centers, that give businesses no-cost help with registrations, certifications, capability statements, and bids.
  2. In the private sector: Government contracting advisory services.Synonyms: business advisory center, small business consultant

References: 49, 56

Apportionment

noun Budget and finance
  1. In government: The Office of Management and Budget’s distribution of appropriated amounts, by time period or by project, that limits what an agency may obligate.
  2. In the private sector: A budget release schedule or spending plan.Synonyms: funding release, budget phasing

References: 67

Appropriation

noun Budget and finance
  1. In government: A law that provides budget authority, letting agencies incur obligations and make payments for specified purposes.
  2. In the private sector: Approved budget or funding.Synonyms: budget allocation, funding approval

References: 67

B

Business Registration Certificate (BRC)

noun Contracting

Also: BRC, NJ-REG

  1. In government: New Jersey’s proof that a business is registered with the Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services. Every vendor needs a valid BRC before a State contract is awarded.
  2. In the private sector: Proof of business registration for a customer’s vendor file.

References: 45

C

CAGE code

noun Contracting

Also: Commercial and Government Entity code, NCAGE

  1. In government: A five-character Commercial and Government Entity code that the Defense Logistics Agency assigns to entities in the United States and its territories.
  2. In the private sector: Supplier location ID.Synonyms: supplier code

References: 9

Capability statement

noun Contracting
  1. In government: A one-page summary of a business written for government buyers and prime contractors, organized around core competencies, differentiators, past performance, company data, and contact information.
  2. In the private sector: Company one-pager or sell sheet.Synonyms: company profile, line card

References: 2, 58

Certification (civil service)

noun New Jersey public service
  1. In government: A list of names, in rank order, that the Civil Service Commission issues from an eligible list when a hiring agency needs to fill a vacancy. Candidates have five business days to respond, and being certified does not guarantee a job.
  2. In the private sector: A shortlist of qualified candidates sent to the hiring manager.Synonyms: candidate slate

References: 41

Chain of command

noun Military
  1. In government: The line of commanders, from senior to junior, through which authority is exercised and orders are passed.
  2. In the private sector: Reporting structure or reporting line.Synonyms: org chart, management hierarchy, line management

Example: “Briefed recommendations up the reporting line to the senior executive” reads better than “briefed the chain of command.”

References: 52

Commander’s intent

noun Military
  1. In government: A short, clear statement of the purpose of an operation and the end state the commander wants, which lets subordinates act on their own when plans change.
  2. In the private sector: The goal and the reason behind it: the “why” in a project brief that lets a team make decisions without asking at every step.Synonyms: desired outcome, guiding objective

References: 52

Company grade, field grade, and general officers

plural noun Military

Also: junior officer, mid-grade officer, flag officer

  1. In government: The three tiers of commissioned officers. Company-grade officers (O-1 to O-3) typically lead units of several dozen to several hundred people. Field-grade officers (O-4 to O-6) lead larger units or serve in senior staff roles. General and flag officers (O-7 to O-10) lead organizations of thousands of people or more.
  2. In the private sector: Roughly: manager (company grade), director or senior director (field grade), and executive (general or flag officer). Let the scope you describe carry the comparison.

References: 3

Competitive and noncompetitive titles (New Jersey)

plural noun New Jersey public service
  1. In government: In New Jersey civil service, competitive titles require passing an examination to be appointed with regular status, while noncompetitive titles do not.
  2. In the private sector: Two hiring tracks. Neither term needs to appear on a private résumé.

References: 39

Competitive service and excepted service

noun Federal workforce

Also: excepted service, Schedule A, Schedule B, Schedule C

  1. In government: Most federal jobs are in the competitive service and are filled through competitive examination under OPM rules. Excepted-service positions are excepted from those procedures and fall into Schedules A through D or are excepted by statute.
  2. In the private sector: Two hiring tracks with different rules. On a private résumé, neither term needs to appear.

References: 13

Continuing resolution (CR)

noun Budget and finance

Also: CR

  1. In government: An appropriation act that keeps agencies funded until their regular appropriations are enacted.
  2. In the private sector: A temporary budget extension.Synonyms: stopgap budget, interim budget

References: 67

Contracting officer (CO)

noun Contracting

Also: CO, KO, warrant

  1. In government: A federal official with authority to enter into, administer, and terminate contracts on the government’s behalf.
  2. In the private sector: Procurement manager or contracts manager with signing authority.Synonyms: procurement officer, purchasing manager, buyer

References: 22

Contracting officer’s representative (COR)

noun Contracting

Also: COR, COTR

  1. In government: A government employee designated in writing by the contracting officer to act within stated limits, usually for technical oversight. A COR cannot make changes that affect price, quality, quantity, delivery, or other contract terms.
  2. In the private sector: Vendor manager or contract administrator.Synonyms: contract manager, vendor relationship manager

Example: “Managed vendor performance on a $3 million services contract against service-level standards.”

References: 15

Course of action (COA)

noun Military

Also: COA, COA analysis

  1. In government: Any sequence of activities that an individual or unit may follow. Planners develop and compare several before a decision.
  2. In the private sector: An option or alternative in a decision memo or business case.Synonyms: scenario, proposal

Example: “Developed and compared three options and recommended the lowest-cost one to the director.”

References: 52

CPARS

proper noun Contracting

Also: contractor performance assessment

  1. In government: The Contractor Performance Assessment Reporting System, where agencies record evaluations of contractor performance.
  2. In the private sector: Formal client performance reviews.Synonyms: client review, vendor scorecard, supplier rating

References: 7

Credentialing Opportunities On-Line (COOL)

proper noun Military records and benefits

Also: COOL, Army COOL, Navy COOL

  1. In government: Service programs that help members identify, and in many cases pay for, civilian licenses and certifications that match their military experience.
  2. In the private sector: Industry certifications. Earn and list them before you separate where you can.Synonyms: professional certification, industry credential, license

References: 57

D

DD Form 214

proper noun Military records and benefits

Also: DD-214, discharge papers, separation document

  1. In government: The Certificate of Release or Discharge from Active Duty. It is generally issued after active duty or at least 90 consecutive days of active duty training and is used to verify service for benefits, retirement, and employment. Veterans can get free copies from the National Archives.
  2. In the private sector: Proof of military service. Keep copies ready for veterans’ preference, benefits, and employers who verify service, and do not pay a company to obtain it.

References: 36, 37

E

Eligible list

noun New Jersey public service

Also: employment list

  1. In government: The ranked list of candidates who passed a civil service examination for a title. State and local agencies use it to fill vacancies.
  2. In the private sector: A ranked candidate pool or talent pipeline.Synonyms: candidate slate, hiring pool

References: 40

End state

noun Military
  1. In government: The set of conditions that, once met, show the commander’s objectives have been achieved.
  2. In the private sector: Definition of done, success criteria, or target outcome.Synonyms: deliverable, milestone

References: 52

Evaluation reports (OER, NCOER, FITREP, EVAL)

plural noun Military

Also: OER, NCOER, FITREP, EVAL, OPR, EPR, fitness report

  1. In government: The services’ formal performance reports. The Army uses the Officer Evaluation Report and the NCO Evaluation Report. The Navy uses fitness reports and evaluations for officers, chiefs, and other enlisted members.
  2. In the private sector: Annual performance reviews. Mine them for numbers, rankings, and comments you can turn into résumé bullets.Synonyms: appraisal, annual review

References: 11, 38

F

Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR)

proper noun Contracting

Also: FAR, DFARS

  1. In government: The main body of rules for how federal executive agencies buy goods and services.
  2. In the private sector: Procurement policy. FAR experience transfers to government contractors and to corporate procurement.Synonyms: procurement rules, purchasing policy

References: 14

Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS)

proper noun Federal workforce

Also: FERS, Basic Benefit, annuity

  1. In government: The federal retirement plan with three parts: a Basic Benefit Plan (a pension), Social Security, and the Thrift Savings Plan. Social Security and the TSP go with you if you leave federal service.
  2. In the private sector: A pension plus Social Security plus a 401(k)-style plan.Synonyms: pension plan, retirement plan, defined benefit plan

References: 71

Federal résumé

noun Federal workforce

Also: USAJOBS résumé, two-page résumé

  1. In government: The résumé used to apply for a federal job. Since September 27, 2025, agencies hiring under Title 5 accept résumés of no more than two pages. Each job lists the title, the employer, and start and end dates by month and year, plus the series and grade for federal positions. Hours worked per week are now optional.
  2. In the private sector: For private employers, drop series numbers and grades, explain titles in plain words, and turn duties into results.

References: 69

Firm-fixed-price contract

noun Contracting

Also: FFP

  1. In government: A contract whose price is not adjusted based on the contractor’s actual costs.
  2. In the private sector: Fixed-fee or fixed-bid contract.Synonyms: lump-sum contract

References: 19

Fiscal year

noun Budget and finance

Also: FY

  1. In government: The government’s accounting year. The federal fiscal year runs from October 1 through September 30.
  2. In the private sector: Fiscal year, the same term. Note when an organization’s year differs from the calendar year.Synonyms: financial year, budget year

References: 67

G

General Schedule (GS)

proper noun Federal workforce

Also: GS, grade, GS-13

  1. In government: The main pay system for federal white-collar employees, with 15 grades from GS-1 to GS-15 and 10 steps in each grade. Pay varies by location.
  2. In the private sector: Salary band or job level. Give the scope of the role rather than the grade alone.Synonyms: pay band, salary grade

Example: “Program Analyst (GS-12)” becomes “Program Analyst, managed a $6 million grant portfolio.”

References: 72, 76

GSA Multiple Award Schedule

proper noun Contracting

Also: GSA Schedule, MAS

  1. In government: Long-term, government-wide contracts the General Services Administration awards to commercial companies, which agencies use to buy at pre-negotiated prices.
  2. In the private sector: Approved vendor list or pre-negotiated price agreement.Synonyms: approved supplier list, preferred vendor list

References: 64

H

Hatch Act

proper noun Federal workforce
  1. In government: A law that limits the political activity of federal executive branch employees, except the President and Vice President. Most employees are “less restricted,” and some agencies’ employees are “further restricted.” The Office of Special Counsel enforces it.
  2. In the private sector: Rules on political activity at work. Private employers have their own policies, so read them before you assume the same limits apply.Synonyms: political activity policy

References: 81

I

Indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contract (IDIQ)

noun Contracting

Also: IDIQ, task order, delivery order

  1. In government: A contract for an uncertain amount of supplies or services within stated minimum and maximum limits over a fixed period, with the government placing orders as needs arise.
  2. In the private sector: Master services agreement with task orders.Synonyms: master agreement, framework agreement

References: 20

J

Joint

adjective Military

Also: joint staff, joint assignment

  1. In government: Describes activities and organizations in which elements of two or more military departments take part.
  2. In the private sector: Cross-functional or interagency. “Joint staff” experience often means coordinating across separate organizations.Synonyms: cross-departmental, multi-stakeholder, enterprise-wide

References: 52

Joint Services Transcript (JST)

proper noun Military records and benefits

Also: JST, ACE credit, CCAF transcript

  1. In government: A free, academically accepted record of military training and occupational experience, with college credit recommendations from the American Council on Education (ACE). Each college decides which recommendations to accept. Air Force and Space Force members get transcripts from the Community College of the Air Force instead.
  2. In the private sector: Training transcript or continuing education record.Synonyms: training record

References: 55, 57

Locality pay

noun Federal workforce
  1. In government: A geographic adjustment that raises GS pay above the base rate in each pay area.
  2. In the private sector: Cost-of-labor or geographic pay differential.Synonyms: location-based pay

References: 76

Logistics

noun Military
  1. In government: Planning and carrying out the movement and support of forces, including supply, maintenance, transportation, and services.
  2. In the private sector: Supply chain management, procurement, transportation, warehousing, and inventory control.Synonyms: operations, fulfillment, distribution

References: 52, 60

M

Management and Program Analyst (0343)

noun Federal workforce

Also: program analyst, management analyst, program evaluator

  1. In government: A federal analyst who advises managers on how well government programs and operations work and on their productivity and efficiency. OPM’s 2024 update also covers program evaluation work.
  2. In the private sector: Business analyst, operations analyst, management consultant, or program evaluator.Synonyms: process improvement analyst, performance analyst, strategy analyst

References: 77

Merit system principles

noun Federal workforce
  1. In government: The principles in 5 U.S.C. 2301 that require federal hiring and advancement to rest on ability, knowledge, and skills after fair and open competition, along with fair treatment of employees.
  2. In the private sector: Fair, merit-based hiring and promotion practices. HR professionals can present this as compliance and equitable process design.Synonyms: fair hiring practices

References: 28

Military Occupational Specialty (MOS)

noun Military

Also: AFSC, rating, SFSC, MOC

  1. In government: The code and title for a service member’s job. The Army and Marine Corps use MOS codes, the Air Force uses Air Force Specialty Codes (AFSC), the Navy and Coast Guard use ratings, and the Space Force uses Space Force Specialty Codes (SFSC).
  2. In the private sector: Your job title and field. Replace the code with a plain title, and use a crosswalk to find the civilian occupations that match.Synonyms: role, career field

Example: Instead of “92A, Automated Logistical Specialist,” write “Inventory and supply specialist responsible for a $4 million parts account.”

References: 60

Mission

noun Military
  1. In government: The task, together with its purpose, that makes clear what action is to be taken and why.
  2. In the private sector: Objective, assignment, or business goal. Avoid using “mission” for every task on a private-sector résumé.Synonyms: mandate, purpose

References: 52

NAICS code

  1. In government: An industry code from the North American Industry Classification System. The SBA assigns a size standard to each NAICS code, so a company’s codes also determine whether it counts as small.

NIGP commodity code

noun Contracting

Also: commodity code, NJSTART

  1. In government: A commodity and services code from the National Institute of Governmental Purchasing. New Jersey uses five-digit NIGP codes in NJSTART for vendor registration and bid notices.
  2. In the private sector: Product or service category code.Synonyms: product category code

References: 46

Noncommissioned officer (NCO)

noun Military

Also: NCO, petty officer, sergeant, chief

  1. In government: An enlisted leader, such as a sergeant or petty officer, who supervises, trains, and evaluates junior enlisted members and carries out the unit’s daily work.
  2. In the private sector: Supervisor, team lead, or operations manager, depending on the number of people and the scope of the work.Synonyms: shift supervisor, first-line manager, crew lead

References: 30

O

Obligation

noun Budget and finance
  1. In government: A binding agreement, such as a signed contract or order, that will result in payments now or later.
  2. In the private sector: Committed spend or encumbrance.Synonyms: commitment, purchase commitment

Example: “Obligated 99 percent of a $12 million budget by year end” becomes “Committed 99 percent of a $12 million budget on schedule.”

References: 67

Occupational series

noun Federal workforce

Also: job series, 0343, 1102, 2210, 0201

  1. In government: A four-digit code that groups federal positions by line of work, such as 0343 (Management and Program Analysis), 0201 (Human Resources Management), 1102 (Contracting), and 2210 (Information Technology Management). OPM’s handbook defines each series.
  2. In the private sector: Job family. Search the private market by the work itself, not the series number.Synonyms: occupational category, function

References: 74

Open competitive examination

noun New Jersey public service
  1. In government: A New Jersey civil service examination open to the public. Those who pass go on an eligible list ranked by residency, veterans’ status, and final average.
  2. In the private sector: An open external hiring process.Synonyms: external recruitment, open posting

References: 40

Open Public Records Act (OPRA)

proper noun New Jersey public service

Also: OPRA request, records custodian

  1. In government: New Jersey’s public records law. A records custodian must respond as soon as possible, and generally within seven business days of receiving a complete request.
  2. In the private sector: Records management and public information compliance, a skill that transfers to compliance and legal operations roles.

References: 48

Operation order (OPORD)

noun Military

Also: OPORD, FRAGO, warning order

  1. In government: A directive a commander issues to subordinate commanders so that an operation is carried out in a coordinated way.
  2. In the private sector: Project plan, execution plan, or launch brief.Synonyms: project charter, rollout plan, operating plan

References: 52

OSDBU

noun Contracting

Also: Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization, OSBP

  1. In government: An Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization, the small business advocacy office inside a federal agency.
  2. In the private sector: Supplier diversity office.Synonyms: small business liaison

References: 4

Outlay

noun Budget and finance
  1. In government: A payment that settles an obligation.
  2. In the private sector: Cash disbursement or actual spend.Synonyms: expenditure, payment

P

Past performance

noun Contracting

Also: CPARS, references

  1. In government: An evaluation factor that looks at how well an offeror performed on earlier contracts. An offeror with no relevant record may not be rated favorably or unfavorably on it.
  2. In the private sector: Track record and client references.Synonyms: case studies, client track record

References: 7, 18

Pay grade

noun Military

Also: rank, grade, E-5, O-3, W-2

  1. In government: A letter and number that sets a service member’s pay: E for enlisted, W for warrant officer, and O for commissioned officer. The rank title that goes with each grade differs by service.
  2. In the private sector: Level of responsibility. Employers rarely know what an E-6 or O-3 does, so describe the size of the team, budget, and mission you led.Synonyms: job level, seniority level, pay band

Example: “Supervised 14 technicians and $2.1 million in equipment” tells more than “Staff Sergeant (E-6).”

References: 3, 30

Performance work statement (PWS)

noun Contracting

Also: PWS, SOW, statement of work

  1. In government: A statement of work that describes required results rather than methods or hours, with measurable performance standards.
  2. In the private sector: Scope of work with service-level agreements.

References: 23

Permanent change of station (PCS)

noun Military

Also: PCS, permanent duty station, PDS

  1. In government: An assignment to a new permanent duty station, with the move governed by travel regulations.
  2. In the private sector: Relocation or transfer. Frequent moves explain short tenures; a brief note such as “military relocation” answers the question before it is asked.Synonyms: reassignment

References: 10

PERS, PFRS, and TPAF

plural proper noun New Jersey public service

Also: PERS, PFRS, TPAF, DCRP, pension

  1. In government: New Jersey’s main public pension systems, administered by the Division of Pensions and Benefits: the Public Employees’ Retirement System, the Police and Firemen’s Retirement System, and the Teachers’ Pension and Annuity Fund. Membership in PERS is mandatory for most eligible employees.
  2. In the private sector: Defined benefit pensions. Before leaving, ask the Division what happens to your service credit and contributions.Synonyms: pension plan, defined benefit plan

References: 43, 44

Position description (PD)

noun Federal workforce

Also: PD

  1. In government: The official statement of the major duties, responsibilities, and supervisory relationships assigned to a federal position. Its duties drive the classification of the job into a series and grade.
  2. In the private sector: Job description. Your PD is a good starting list of duties, but rewrite it into results.Synonyms: role profile

References: 75

Post-9/11 GI Bill (Chapter 33)

proper noun Military records and benefits

Also: Chapter 33, GI Bill

  1. In government: A VA education benefit for people who served on active duty after September 10, 2001. Depending on eligibility, it can cover tuition and fees, a housing allowance, and books and supplies.
  2. In the private sector: Education benefits. Employers may ask about degree plans; the benefit itself does not belong on a résumé.Synonyms: tuition assistance

References: 63

Post-employment restrictions (New Jersey)

noun Ethics and leaving government

Also: Conflicts of Interest Law, revolving door

  1. In government: Under New Jersey’s Conflicts of Interest Law, a former State officer or employee may not represent or assist anyone on a particular matter in which they were substantially and directly involved. Certain senior officials, such as department heads and their deputies, also may not represent anyone other than the State before their former agency for one year.
  2. In the private sector: A lifetime, matter-specific restriction. Contact the State Ethics Commission or your agency’s ethics liaison with questions before you start a new role.Synonyms: non-compete, conflict-of-interest rule

References: 50, 51

Post-government employment restrictions (federal)

noun Ethics and leaving government

Also: 18 U.S.C. 207, revolving door, cooling-off period

  1. In government: Limits under 18 U.S.C. 207 on what former executive branch employees may do after leaving. They include a lifetime ban on representing others before the government on particular matters involving specific parties that you worked on personally and substantially, a two-year ban on such matters that were under your official responsibility, and a one-year cooling-off period for senior employees.
  2. In the private sector: Restrictions similar to a non-compete for public service. Ask your agency ethics official before accepting work that touches your former agency.Synonyms: revolving-door rules

References: 59, 68

Promotional examination

noun New Jersey public service
  1. In government: A civil service examination for promotion. Promotional lists rank candidates by a combination of testing mode, seniority, and performance evaluation. Veterans are ranked by score like everyone else, but a veteran at the top of a promotional certification has preference over non-veterans.
  2. In the private sector: An internal promotion process.Synonyms: internal posting

References: 40, 42

R

Readiness

noun Military
  1. In government: The ability of military forces to fight and meet the demands of their assigned missions.
  2. In the private sector: Operational preparedness. Readiness reporting resembles tracking equipment availability, staffing levels, and training completion against targets.Synonyms: operational readiness, uptime

Example: “Raised equipment availability from 78 to 94 percent in one year.”

References: 52

Reduction in force (RIF)

noun Federal workforce

Also: RIF, layoff, retention register

  1. In government: A federal layoff. The law requires OPM’s retention rules to give effect to tenure, veterans’ preference, length of service, and performance, and the rule that governs notices issued on or after September 2, 2026, changes how those factors are scored.
  2. In the private sector: Layoff or restructuring. On a résumé, “position eliminated in an agency reorganization” is accurate and enough.Synonyms: downsizing, workforce reduction

References: 1, 78

Request for information (RFI)

noun Contracting

Also: RFI, sources sought, market research

  1. In government: A notice an agency issues to gather price, delivery, market, or capability information for planning when it does not yet intend to award a contract. Responses are not offers.
  2. In the private sector: Market research request.Synonyms: market survey, vendor survey

References: 16

Request for proposals (RFP)

noun Contracting

Also: RFP, RFQ, solicitation

  1. In government: The solicitation an agency uses in a negotiated acquisition to communicate its requirements and ask for proposals.
  2. In the private sector: RFP, the same term in the private sector.Synonyms: request for quote, tender, bid request

References: 17

Rule of Three

proper noun New Jersey public service
  1. In government: A New Jersey civil service rule that lets a hiring agency appoint any one of the top three interested eligibles on a certification. A veteran among the top three changes how the rule applies.
  2. In the private sector: Choosing among the top three finalists.

References: 41, 42

S

SAM.gov registration

noun Contracting

Also: System for Award Management

  1. In government: Registration in the System for Award Management, which offerors must hold when they submit offers for most federal contracts. It must be renewed every 365 days.
  2. In the private sector: Vendor registration with a customer.Synonyms: vendor onboarding, supplier registration

References: 2, 24, 66

Schedule A (disability hiring authority)

proper noun Federal workforce

Also: Schedule A letter, 213.3102(u)

  1. In government: An excepted-service authority (5 CFR 213.3102(u)) that lets agencies hire people with intellectual disabilities, severe physical disabilities, or psychiatric disabilities without competition. Employees may qualify for conversion to permanent status after two years of satisfactory service.
  2. In the private sector: A federal hiring pathway, not a private-sector term.

References: 13, 70

Security clearance

noun Military

Also: Secret, Top Secret, TS/SCI, continuous vetting

  1. In government: A determination that a person is eligible for access to classified information. The three general levels are Confidential, Secret, and Top Secret, and access also requires a need to know. DCSA is moving from periodic reinvestigations to continuous vetting.
  2. In the private sector: A valuable credential for defense contractors and some government roles. List the level and whether it is active, and never list classified details.

Example: “Active Secret clearance” in the summary or skills section.

References: 5, 8

Senior Executive Service (SES)

proper noun Federal workforce

Also: SES, ES

  1. In government: The corps of federal managerial, supervisory, and policy positions above GS-15, created by the Civil Service Reform Act of 1978.
  2. In the private sector: Executive leadership, comparable to vice president or senior vice president roles depending on scope.Synonyms: senior leadership team, executive team

References: 79

Set-aside

noun Contracting

Also: small business set-aside, rule of two

  1. In government: A contract reserved for a class of businesses, such as small businesses, service-disabled veteran-owned small businesses, or, in New Jersey, certified disabled veterans’ businesses.
  2. In the private sector: Reserved bidding pool for qualified suppliers.Synonyms: supplier diversity program, reserved contract

References: 21, 34, 83

SkillBridge

proper noun Military records and benefits

Also: DOD SkillBridge, DOW SkillBridge

  1. In government: A Department of Defense program, now branded DOW SkillBridge, that lets service members gain civilian work experience through industry training, apprenticeships, or internships during their last 180 days of service, with their commander’s approval. Participants continue to receive military pay and benefits.
  2. In the private sector: An employer-hosted internship or fellowship. List it as work experience with the host company’s name.Synonyms: apprenticeship, work placement

References: 27, 29

Sole-source award

noun Contracting

Also: other than full and open competition, J&A

  1. In government: A contract awarded without full and open competition, allowed only in circumstances the regulation lists.
  2. In the private sector: Single-source procurement.Synonyms: direct award, no-bid contract

References: 25, 83

Specialized experience

noun Federal workforce
  1. In government: Experience that has given an applicant the particular knowledge, skills, and abilities to do the job. At GS-5 and above, most positions require one year of it at a level equivalent to at least the next lower grade, and each job announcement describes what counts.
  2. In the private sector: Required qualifications or must-have experience.Synonyms: relevant experience, minimum qualifications

References: 73

Standard operating procedure (SOP)

noun Military

Also: SOP, standing operating procedure

  1. In government: A set of instructions for the parts of an operation that can follow a set procedure without losing effectiveness. It is also called a standing operating procedure.
  2. In the private sector: Standard operating procedure, process documentation, or standard work. This one translates almost directly.Synonyms: playbook, runbook, work instruction

References: 52

Step and within-grade increase

noun Federal workforce

Also: WGI, step increase, step 10

  1. In government: Pay levels inside a GS grade. Employees with acceptable performance move up after waiting periods of one year (steps 1 to 3), two years (steps 4 to 6), and three years (steps 7 to 9), so reaching step 10 normally takes 18 years in one grade.
  2. In the private sector: A seniority-based raise schedule. In private salary talks, convert your total pay, including locality pay, into an annual figure.Synonyms: seniority raise, pay progression

References: 72

T

Temporary duty (TDY)

noun Military

Also: TDY, TAD, per diem

  1. In government: Travel away from the permanent duty station for a set period, followed by a return. Travel orders and per diem rules govern it.
  2. In the private sector: Business travel or a temporary assignment.Synonyms: work trip, secondment

References: 10

Thrift Savings Plan (TSP)

proper noun Federal workforce

Also: TSP

  1. In government: The defined contribution retirement savings plan for federal employees and members of the uniformed services, with agency contributions for FERS employees.
  2. In the private sector: A 401(k). Learn your options for leaving it in place or rolling it over before you change jobs.Synonyms: defined contribution plan, retirement savings plan

References: 26, 71

Transition Assistance Program (TAP)

proper noun Military records and benefits

Also: TAP, transition, capstone

  1. In government: The required counseling and training that prepares service members to leave the military. It is mandatory for members with at least 180 continuous days of active duty and must begin no later than 365 days before separation. Retirees are advised to start at least two years before retirement.
  2. In the private sector: Career transition and outplacement services.Synonyms: career transition services

References: 31, 53

U

Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200)

proper noun Budget and finance

Also: 2 CFR 200, Super Circular, cost principles

  1. In government: The federal rules for administering grants and other federal awards, including cost principles and audit requirements.
  2. In the private sector: Grants compliance. Valued by nonprofits, universities, and health systems that manage federal funds.Synonyms: grant compliance, federal grant rules

References: 12

Unique Entity ID (UEI)

noun Contracting

Also: UEI, DUNS

  1. In government: The 12-character identifier that SAM.gov assigns to an organization. It replaced the DUNS number as the federal government’s authoritative entity identifier on April 4, 2022.
  2. In the private sector: Vendor or supplier ID.Synonyms: vendor number

References: 65

V

VA disability rating

noun Military records and benefits

Also: service-connected disability, combined rating

  1. In government: A percentage, from 0 to 100 in steps of 10, that the Department of Veterans Affairs assigns to service-connected conditions. Multiple ratings are combined with a table rather than added together.
  2. In the private sector: Personal benefit information, not résumé content. It matters for veterans’ preference and some federal and state hiring authorities.

References: 61

Veteran Readiness and Employment (VR&E, Chapter 31)

proper noun Military records and benefits

Also: VR&E, Chapter 31, voc rehab

  1. In government: A VA program, formerly called Vocational Rehabilitation and Employment, that helps veterans with service-connected disabilities prepare for, find, and keep suitable jobs.
  2. In the private sector: Career retraining support. Using VR&E does not reduce other VA education benefits.Synonyms: workforce retraining, career services

References: 62

Veterans Employment Opportunities Act (VEOA)

proper noun Federal workforce

Also: VEOA

  1. In government: A law that lets eligible veterans apply for federal jobs that an agency announces under its merit promotion procedures and opens to candidates from outside its own workforce.
  2. In the private sector: A federal hiring pathway, not a private-sector term.

References: 80

Veterans’ preference (federal)

noun Federal workforce

Also: 5-point, 10-point, SF-15, preference eligible

  1. In government: An advantage in federal hiring for eligible veterans. There are three types: sole survivorship (0-point), non-disabled (5-point), and disabled (10-point) preference.
  2. In the private sector: No private-sector equivalent. Some private employers have voluntary veteran hiring programs instead.

References: 80

Veterans’ preference (New Jersey)

noun New Jersey public service

Also: absolute preference

  1. In government: New Jersey gives absolute preference on open competitive lists: disabled veterans, then veterans, are placed above other candidates who pass, regardless of score. Veterans apply for preference through the New Jersey Department of Veterans Affairs.
  2. In the private sector: No private-sector equivalent. It is a public employment rule only.

References: 32, 42

Veterans Recruitment Appointment (VRA)

proper noun Federal workforce

Also: VRA

  1. In government: An excepted-service authority that lets an agency appoint an eligible veteran without competition. After two years of satisfactory service, the veteran can be converted to the competitive service.
  2. In the private sector: A direct-hire pathway, not a private-sector term.

References: 80

VOB and DVOB (New Jersey)

plural noun Contracting

Also: veteran-owned business, disabled veteran-owned business

  1. In government: New Jersey’s veteran-owned and disabled veteran-owned business certifications. Agencies must give due consideration to VOBs and aim to award at least 3 percent of contracts to disabled veterans’ businesses.
  2. In the private sector: State supplier diversity certifications.Synonyms: diverse supplier certification

References: 2, 33, 34, 35, 47

VOSB and SDVOSB

plural noun Contracting

Also: VetCert, service-disabled veteran-owned small business

  1. In government: Veteran-owned and service-disabled veteran-owned small businesses certified by the SBA through VetCert. SDVOSBs can compete for set-aside and sole-source contracts government-wide, and VOSBs can compete for them at the Department of Veterans Affairs. The government-wide SDVOSB goal is 5 percent of contract dollars.
  2. In the private sector: Federal supplier diversity certifications.Synonyms: diverse supplier certification, veteran-owned business certification

References: 6, 83

W

Warrant officer

noun Military

Also: chief warrant officer, CWO, W-2

  1. In government: An officer in pay grades W-1 to W-5 who performs highly technical or specialized work in a career field. In the Army, warrant officers also serve as helicopter pilots.
  2. In the private sector: Senior technical specialist, principal engineer, or subject-matter expert.Synonyms: technical lead, staff engineer

References: 3
